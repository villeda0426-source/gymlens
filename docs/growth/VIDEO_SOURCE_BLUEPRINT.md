# Source Blueprint: “How To Reach 10,000 App Users”

Source: Steven Cravotta, [How To Reach 10,000 App Users (Beginner Marketing)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e_t1gYkvZkI), 17:16.

## What the Video Actually Teaches

The video presents a recurring acquisition loop, not a one-time launch checklist:

1. Identify a message capable of earning broad attention.
2. Research existing high-performing content in the problem category.
3. create more content personally or through creators.
4. Put paid distribution behind content already showing evidence of demand.

The creator frames 10,000 users as a milestone, but does not provide a dated forecast, required reach calculation, retention target, or statistical decision model. Those must be added for Coachlift.

## Scene-by-Scene Reference Map

### 0:00–2:20 — Promise and four-part system

**What is shown**

- The desired users are described as people who provide feedback, engage, use the product, and eventually pay—not arbitrary downloads.
- A four-part “Marketing System” is introduced: viral message, market research, content creation, and paid scaling.
- The speaker argues that acquiring the first users is harder than scaling after a repeatable method is found.

**Underlying lesson**

The goal is a repeatable acquisition mechanism, not a single lucky viral post.

**Coachlift translation**

Track downloads publicly, but evaluate the system on successful first scans, repeat scans, feedback quality, and eventual monetization.

### 2:20–4:25 — Reddit as the immediate first-user channel

**What is shown**

- Reddit communities such as SaaS, SideProject, programming, and related founder spaces.
- Sorting posts by top performance to see which launches and stories earned engagement.
- Comment sections as a source of feedback, not only referral traffic.
- The instruction to be useful and authentic instead of dropping a bare link.

**Underlying lesson**

Borrow a relevant community’s existing distribution while giving members a story, lesson, or useful discussion worth engaging with.

**Coachlift translation**

- Founder communities can evaluate the build and business story.
- Fitness and beginner communities can validate the user problem, but promotion rules are usually stricter.
- An AI system may research rules, draft, and flag risk; it should never mass-post or impersonate Adan.
- Each post needs moderator/community-rule review and human approval.

### 4:25–6:10 — Viral message and the two reference ads

#### Puff Count vape teardown

**What is shown**

- Hook: people use disposable vapes without knowing what is inside.
- A visual teardown reveals the casing, battery, coil, and soaked cotton.
- Puff Count appears for roughly two seconds near the end with a quitting-related CTA.
- The speaker reports approximately 476,000 likes.

**Why the structure works**

- Curiosity gap: the viewer wants to see what is inside.
- Native educational/entertainment value exists without the product.
- The content attracts a problem-aware audience before introducing the app.
- The product resolves the emotional concern created by the content.

**Coachlift analogue**

Open with an unfamiliar or misunderstood machine, reveal what it actually does and one useful setup fact, then show Coachlift briefly as the faster way to get that answer at any machine.

#### Cal AI transformation/aspiration example

**What is shown**

- A fitness-oriented visual leads with a desirable body or training/nutrition outcome.
- The product appears as a natural supporting tool rather than a feature walkthrough.
- The speaker reports roughly 55,000 likes and cites the company as a very large subscription app.

**Why the structure works**

- It sells the desired identity or outcome rather than calorie-recognition features.
- The viewer understands the category before the product appears.

**Coachlift analogue**

The aspiration is not “AI machine recognition.” It is being the person who can walk into the gym and start confidently without looking lost.

### 6:10–7:30 — The funnel drawing

**What is shown**

The speaker draws a descending sequence resembling:

1. Viral video/view
2. App Store visit
3. Download
4. Onboarding
5. Purchase

He argues that each step has one job: move the person to the next step.

**Underlying lesson**

Do not force one short-form post to explain the full product, close the download, teach onboarding, and sell a subscription.

**Coachlift translation**

- Content earns attention and an App Store visit.
- The App Store page earns the download.
- Onboarding earns the first successful scan.
- Product value earns the return visit.
- A future paywall earns the purchase.

The AI system must diagnose the weakest transition instead of blaming every problem on content.

### 7:30–9:15 — Market research by problem keyword

**What is shown**

- TikTok searches such as “quit vaping” and “tracking calories.”
- Results are sorted toward high-performing examples.
- The speaker studies simple, short executions—including text-led posts and videos around 14 seconds.
- The aim is to reuse concepts and structures that already match the market, not to copy footage or wording.

**Underlying lesson**

Great product content often begins with category-native research: what the target viewer already watches, finishes, shares, and comments on.

**Coachlift research vocabulary**

- how to use gym machines
- gym machines for beginners
- first day at the gym
- gym anxiety
- scared to go to the gym
- what does this machine work
- gym machine mistakes
- machine setup
- beginner gym confidence
- returning to the gym

The research unit is not merely a URL. It is a tagged pattern: audience, hook, promise, tension, visual mechanism, duration, reveal, proof, CTA, comments, and adaptation hypothesis.

### 9:15–10:20 — Trend remix example

**What is shown**

- Puff Count is inserted into the “A win is a win” audio/meme format.
- The product integration is short and culturally legible.
- The speaker reports roughly 33,000 views and notes that the sound had been used widely.

**Underlying lesson**

A trend supplies a familiar delivery mechanism; the brand still needs a relevant product truth or audience joke.

**Coachlift translation**

Only use a trend if it can express gym confusion, a small confidence win, a machine reveal, or a founder moment naturally. Trend volume alone is not enough.

### 10:20–14:25 — Creator scaling through Posted

**What is shown**

- Posted’s brand interface and its Deals workflow.
- A brand creates a project overview, rules, creator-location criteria, and inspiration examples.
- Creators submit finished videos with bids; the brand can review before accepting.
- The speaker shows a queue of roughly 30 submissions and says 17 had been accepted.
- Example creator bids and follower counts illustrate that follower count alone does not determine value.
- A separate CPM model pays according to views.
- Downloadable creative files and TikTok Spark Ad authorization are presented as benefits.
- The speaker reports approximately 4,000 downloads in a week, around $1,500 spent, and roughly $0.37 per download. One spoken line says “views,” but the surrounding claim and calculation clearly refer to downloads.

**Underlying lesson**

Once a brand has a credible brief and example concepts, creators provide parallel creative variation and their own distribution. Commercial structure can reduce—but never eliminate—creative risk.

**Important caveats**

- The speaker promotes a platform with which he appears closely associated; performance claims are first-party claims in promotional content.
- “Risk-free” is marketing language. Time, review effort, brand risk, usage rights, audience quality, and conversion quality remain real risks.
- A bid accepted after submission reduces production-selection risk, while CPM shifts payment toward reach; neither guarantees activated users.
- Usage rights, exclusivity, edit rights, duration, territories, music licensing, likeness releases, disclosure, and ad authorization must be explicit in the agreement.
- Posted is an option, not the architecture. The Coachlift system must allow direct outreach and other marketplaces.

### 14:25–16:35 — Organic winners into paid ads

**What is shown**

- The selection rule is based on both attention and results: which videos get views and which drive installs.
- The speaker explains that paid placement adds a direct CTA and removes some friction from organic discovery.
- RevenueCat’s Realized LTV per Customer chart is used to compare value per download/customer with acquisition cost.
- Puff Count is given as an example of approximately $1.00–$1.10 realized LTV per download/customer; the proposed rule is to acquire below that value.

**Underlying lesson**

Organic distribution is a creative test bed. Paid media amplifies proven creative when unit economics support it.

**Coachlift caveats**

- Views alone do not make a paid winner.
- Gross revenue, net revenue, and proceeds are different. The economic ceiling should use conservative proceeds or contribution value, not an optimistic gross number.
- Mature cohorts matter; recent realized-LTV cohorts are incomplete.
- Coachlift’s free launch may not yet have meaningful LTV, so early ads require a capped learning budget and activation/retention guardrails.

### 16:35–17:16 — The system is continuous

**What is shown**

- Continuous message discovery.
- Continuous trend and market research.
- Continuous creator testing.
- Continuous paid-ad testing and scaling.

**Underlying lesson**

The durable asset is the learning loop and creative library, not one campaign.

## The Examples as Transferable Patterns

| Video reference | Transferable pattern | Coachlift application |
|---|---|---|
| Puff Count teardown | Curiosity-led education, product appears at resolution | Reveal a misunderstood machine and show Coachlift as the instant explainer |
| Cal AI fitness creative | Desired identity/outcome before product | Sell confidence and belonging in the gym |
| Funnel drawing | One job per step | Diagnose content, App Store, onboarding, product, and paywall separately |
| TikTok keyword searches | Research the audience’s content neighborhood | Build a tagged swipe file from beginner-gym and machine-help searches |
| “A win is a win” remix | Express product truth through a native trend | Use trends only when the gym-beginner insight fits naturally |
| Posted Deals | Parallel creator variations from a structured brief | Test multiple gym/beginner creators with controlled rights and approval |
| Spark Ads | Amplify creator-native proof | Advertise authorized creator winners after install and activation validation |
| RevenueCat chart | Compare acquisition cost with customer value | Scale only below a conservative economic ceiling after cohort maturity |

## Claims, Evidence, and Verification Status

| Claim in video | Status | Operating treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit can generate early users and feedback | Plausible channel strategy; results vary | Test manually with rule-compliant, value-first posts |
| Puff Count example earned ~476K likes | Shown/reported in the source video | Treat as illustrative, not a Coachlift forecast |
| Cal AI example earned ~55K likes | Shown/reported in the source video | Treat as illustrative |
| Posted campaign drove ~4K downloads for ~$1.5K | First-party promotional claim | Require Coachlift’s own attribution before accepting comparable economics |
| Posted offers Deals and creator submissions/bids | Supported by Posted’s current public site | Candidate tool, not required infrastructure |
| TikTok Spark Ads use creator authorization | Supported by TikTok’s official documentation | Require explicit, time-bounded authorization and rights review |
| RevenueCat Realized LTV per Customer can inform acquisition profitability | Supported by RevenueCat documentation | Use mature cohorts and conservative proceeds/value assumptions |

## What the Video Does Not Supply

- A calculation connecting reach, App Store conversion, activation, retention, and 10,000 users.
- A content-quality rubric or minimum sample size for declaring a winner.
- A system of record for storing research and experiment history.
- A method for separating correlation from incremental lift.
- Creator contracts, disclosure rules, brand-safety checks, or music-rights procedures.
- App Store optimization instructions.
- Activation and retention improvement instructions.
- A precise automation architecture.
- A human-approval model.

Those missing pieces are added in Coachlift’s AI-assisted system blueprint.
